**Research Title**  
AI‑Based Detection of Fake Internship Offers  

---

### 1. Introduction  

Online internship listings have become a primary gateway for students and early‑career professionals to obtain work experience. University career portals, public job‑board websites, and social‑media groups collectively host thousands of postings each year. At the same time, large language models (LLMs) enable malicious actors to generate highly convincing job descriptions, application forms, and correspondence at minimal cost. Media reports and student‑support services have documented cases in which applicants receive bogus offers that request personal data, fees, or unpaid labour for dubious entities.  

Current detection mechanisms rely on manual verification (e.g., contacting the employer) and simple rule‑based keyword filters. These approaches are increasingly inadequate against the linguistic sophistication of AI‑generated scams, creating a gap for a reliable, scalable, AI‑driven detection solution.  

---

### 2. Problem Statement  

There is no reliable, scalable method that leverages artificial‑intelligence techniques to automatically differentiate authentic internship postings from those that are fabricated or maliciously generated by AI, leading to continued exposure of job‑seekers to fraud.  

---

### 3. Why the Problem Matters  

| Impact Area | Rationale |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Student and early‑career risk** | Victims may suffer financial loss, identity theft, reputational damage, and missed genuine opportunities. |
| **Institutional credibility** | Universities and career services risk losing trust if they cannot safeguard their platforms. |
| **Economic impact** | Fraudulent internships divert time, money, and data that could otherwise be invested in legitimate talent pipelines. |
| **Ethical AI concerns** | The misuse of generative AI for deception undermines public confidence in AI technologies. |

---
